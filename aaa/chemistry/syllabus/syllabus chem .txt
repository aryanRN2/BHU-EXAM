Semester 1
Basic Principles of Chemistry and Its Applications in Daily Life
CHEMD11
1. Basic Principles of Chemistry: Atoms, molecules, and their electronic structures. Types 
of Chemical bonding. Basic concept of Hybridization. Energetics of bond formation, 
Types of intermolecular/intramolecular forces, and molecular association for the 
formation of materials.              
                                                                                                
2. Chemical Reactions: Thermodynamic basis of chemical changes. Enthalpy, entropy, 
and free energy change during a chemical reaction. Types of Chemical Reactions. 
The concept of oxidation number, Reducing and oxidizing agents.    
 
3. Applications of Chemistry in daily life : 
a) Dyes and pigments: General idea about the chemistry of dyes, Classification of dyes, Origin of the colors in the dyes and pigments, and application in garment and printing industries.                                                                                                                   
 b) Polymers: Natural and synthetic polymers, synthesis, properties, and applications. 
(Examples: polyester, rubber, nylon, bakelite, etc). Introduction to biopolymers. 
Application of synthetic polymers in surgical treatments and transplantations. 
c) Food and beverage industry: use of preservatives, food adulteration, flavor 
enhancers, artificial sweetening agents, and antioxidants. 
d) Fragrance: Basic principles, types of fragrances, and their applications. 
e) Cleansing agents: Soaps, detergents, Surfactants, and underlying chemistry of cleansing agents.

CHEMJ11

Eigenvalues and Eigenfunctions, significance of Ψ and Ψ2 Radial and angular wave

A brief introduction to the modern periodic table (Block wise). Atomic size (Covalent,

Standard electrode potential and its determination. Electrochemical series and its 
applications Latimer and Frost diagrams; specific illustrative examples.

Kinetic theory of gases and ideal gas laws, Maxwell distribution of speed, Equipartition 
of Energy collisions in a gas: collision number, collision diameter and mean free path, 
behaviour of real gases: van der Waals equation and its applicability, the critical state: 
critical constants, law of corresponding states. 
 
4. Liquid State: 
Surface tension, capillary action, measurement of surface tension, viscosity of liquids 
and its measurement, temperature effect on surface tension and viscosity 
 
5. Solid State: 
Crystal lattices, space lattice, unit cell, crystal systems, law of rational indices, Miller 
indices, Bragg's equation, crystal structure of NaCl, graphite and diamond. 
 
6. Chemical Thermodynamics and its Applications: 
First law of Thermodynamics, Thermodynamic quantities (w, q, ΔU and ΔH) for 
isothermal and adiabatic reversible expansion of ideal gases, Kirchoff's relations, Joule 
effect, Joule-Thomson effect, Joule-Thomson coefficient of real (van der Waals) gases. 
Second Law of Thermodynamics, Carnot cycle, concept of entropy and its physical
significance, entropy changes for an ideal gas, entropy changes at constant pressure and 
volume, entropy change in spontaneous processes, free energy and maximum work 
functions, conditions for spontaneous changes and equilibrium, Gibbs-Helmholtz 
equation, Maxwell relations. Statement of third law of Thermodynamics
