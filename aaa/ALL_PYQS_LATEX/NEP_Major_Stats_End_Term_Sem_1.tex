\noindent   \textbf{Question 1}   \textit{(Compulsory)}

\begin{parts}
  \item What are the two senses in which the term Statistics is generally used?\pts{3.5}
  \item Define the terms: frequency, relative frequency, cumulative frequency (less than and more than type), and frequency density.\pts{3.5}
  \item The weights (in kg) of 8 persons are: $62.65, 64.92, 64.01, 121.22, 63.11, 69.01, 67.19$, and $72.64$. Find the average weight using a suitable form of average. Give reason for your choice.\pts{3.5}
  \item If the correlation coefficient between $x$ and $y$ is $0.5$, what would be the correlation coefficient between $5x$ and $-3y$?\pts{3.5}
\end{parts}

\medskip\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\medskip




\noindent   \textbf{Question 2}

\begin{parts}
  \item Define mean, median, and mode with their merits and demerits. Also discuss their specific use with suitable examples. Show that the sum of deviation taken from mean is always zero.\pts{9}
  \item The mean age of a group of 100 children is $9.35$ years. The mean age of 25 of them is $8.75$ years and that of another 65 is $10.51$ years. What is the mean age of the remainder?\pts{5}
\end{parts}

\medskip\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\medskip




\noindent   \textbf{Question 3}

\begin{parts}
  \item If $x_1$ and $x_2$ are two positive values of a variate, prove that their geometric mean is equal to the geometric mean of their arithmetic and harmonic means. If the arithmetic mean of two observations is $25$ and their geometric mean is $15$, find their harmonic mean and the two observations.\pts{7}
  \item You are given the following incomplete frequency distribution. It is known that the total frequency is $1000$ and the median is $413.11$. Find the missing frequencies and first quartile:
  
\begin{center}
  
\begin{tabular}{ccccccccc}
     oprule
   \textbf{Values} & 300--325 & 325--350 & 350--375 & 375--400 & 400--425 & 425--450 & 450--475 & 475--500 \\
    \midrule
   \textbf{Frequency} & 5 & 17 & 80 & ? & 326 & ? & 88 & 9 \\
    ottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}\pts{7}
\end{parts}

\medskip\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\medskip




\noindent   \textbf{Question 4}

\begin{parts}
  \item Prove that the standard deviation calculated from two values $x_1$ and $x_2$ of a variable $x$ is equal to half of their absolute difference.\pts{4.5}
  \item Find the standard deviation of the first $n$ natural numbers.\pts{4.5}
  \item The mean and variance of $n$ values $x_1, x_2, x_3, \dots, x_n$ are $0$ and $\sigma^2$ respectively. If $y$ is a variable defined as $y = x^2$, then show that $ar{y} = \sigma^2$.\pts{5}
\end{parts}

\medskip\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\medskip




\noindent   \textbf{Question 5}

\begin{parts}
  \item Out of 400 observations, 100 observations have the value one and the rest of observations are zero. Find the standard deviation of 400 observations together.\pts{3.5}
  \item The mean of a certain distribution is $5$. The second and third moment about the mean is $20$ and $140$ respectively. Find the third moment of the distribution about $10$.\pts{3.5}
  \item If the first quartile is $142$ and inter quartile range is $18$, then find median assuming the distribution is symmetrical.\pts{3.5}
  \item Explain both the measure of Kurtosis and its different types.\pts{3.5}
\end{parts}

\medskip\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\medskip




\noindent   \textbf{Question 6}

\begin{parts}
  \item Calculate the correlation coefficient and its probable error from the following results ($n = 40$):
  \[ \sum_{i=1}^{40} x_i = 280, \quad \sum_{i=1}^{40} y_i = 60, \quad \sum_{i=1}^{40} x_i^2 = 2384, \quad \sum_{i=1}^{40} y_i^2 = 117, \quad \sum_{i=1}^{40} x_i y_i = 438 \] \pts{7}
  \item What are regression lines? Explain why we have two regression lines and why these two lines are identical if correlation coefficient is $+1$ or $-1$. Also show that the correlation coefficient is geometric mean of the regression coefficients.\pts{7}
\end{parts}

\medskip\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\medskip




\noindent   \textbf{Question 7}

\begin{parts}
  \item In the following table, the recorded data shows the test scores made by 10 salesmen on an IQ test and their weekly sales:
  
\begin{center}
  
\begin{tabular}{ccccccccccc}
     oprule
   \textbf{Tests Scores on IQ} & 50 & 70 & 50 & 60 & 80 & 50 & 90 & 50 & 60 & 60 \\
    \midrule
   \textbf{Weekly Sales (in '000 Rs.)} & 25 & 60 & 45 & 50 & 45 & 20 & 60 & 30 & 45 & 40 \\
    ottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  Calculate the Rank correlation coefficient between IQ and efficiency in sales.\pts{5}
  \item If $A$ and $B$ are independent attributes, $N = 24$, $(A) = 16$ and $(B) = 6$, find the ultimate class frequencies.\pts{4.5}
  \item In a college out of 200 students 150 are boys. In an examination 120 boys and 40 girls passed. Find Yule's coefficient of association between gender and success, also comment on the result obtained.\pts{4.5}
\end{parts}
